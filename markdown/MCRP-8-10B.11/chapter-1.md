# Chapter 1: Antenna Fundamentals

## 1.1 Purpose and Scope
This chapter outlines the basic physics and terminology necessary to understand tactical antennas. It frames how wavelength, frequency, polarization, and radiation patterns influence the ability to send and receive information under expeditionary conditions.

## 1.2 Electromagnetic Waves
Radio waves propagate as electromagnetic energy traveling at the speed of light. Frequency \(f\) and wavelength \(\lambda\) relate by \(\lambda = c / f\). Lower frequencies (HF) support long-range skywave propagation, while higher frequencies (VHF/UHF) excel at line-of-sight and satellite links.

## 1.3 Polarization
The orientation of the electric field defines polarization. Matching transmit and receive polarization maximizes signal strength. Tactical systems commonly use vertical polarization for omnidirectional coverage and horizontal or circular polarization for directional or satellite links.

## 1.4 Radiation Patterns
Radiation patterns depict how power is distributed in space. Key characteristics include main lobes, side lobes, and nulls. Omnidirectional vertical monopoles provide 360° azimuth coverage with elevation nulls, while dipoles and directional arrays concentrate energy to improve gain and reduce interference.

## 1.5 Feed Lines and Impedance
Coaxial cable, ladder line, and waveguide convey RF energy between radios and antennas. A 50-ohm impedance standard minimizes mismatch loss for tactical equipment. Proper connectors, weatherproofing, and strain relief preserve performance in the field.

## 1.6 Grounding and Safety
Effective grounding shunts static and lightning energy away from personnel and electronics. Use ground rods, bonding straps, and surge protectors whenever feasible. Maintain safe distances from radiating elements to avoid RF burns and induced currents in nearby metallic objects.

## 1.7 Practical Takeaways
- Choose antenna types based on mission range, terrain, and required bandwidth.
- Keep feed lines as short as practicable and protect them from abrasion and moisture.
- Survey surrounding structures and power lines before erecting any radiating system.
